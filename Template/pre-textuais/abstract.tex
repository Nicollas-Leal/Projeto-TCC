\dabstract{
This study conducts a systematic literature review on the development of systems and interfaces aimed at people with low computer literacy. The study seeks to identify and analyze, in the scientific literature, metrics, design principles, guidelines, and interface components related to usability, accessibility, and human-computer interaction for this audience. The research was conducted through searches in the Scopus, IEEE Xplore, and Engineering Village databases, followed by filtering, title and abstract screening, and full-text reading according to previously established inclusion and exclusion criteria. The analysis of the selected studies seeks to synthesize the main approaches used to facilitate interaction for users with limited computer experience, as well as the resources employed in the evaluation and development of these interfaces. Thus, the study contributes to organizing existing knowledge on the subject and identifying elements that may support future research on the development of more accessible, intuitive, and inclusive interfaces.
}
